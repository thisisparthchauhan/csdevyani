Operational Readiness

Once Alternative Investment Fund Registration is granted, SEBI expects AIFs to function within a structured governance framework. Before commencing fund-raising, the AIF must ensure:

  • Finalisation of constitutive documents & PPM
  • Appointment of key functionaries
  • Operational bank & escrow accounts
  • Robust record-keeping & reporting systems
  • Regulatory readiness before first investor onboarding

Why Ongoing Compliance Matters

AIFs operate in a trust-based regulatory environment. Non-compliance can attract regulatory scrutiny, monetary penalties, or restrictions on fund operations.

Core Post-Registration Obligations

Fund Raising & Placement

Private placement only. Issue final PPM, ensure min investment thresholds, and strictly no public solicitation.

Investor Onboarding

Strict KYC/AML checks, verification of eligibility, and accurate documentation of commitments.

Reporting & Disclosures

Quarterly reports to SEBI, annual compliance confirmations, and transparent investment disclosures.

Valuation

Follow PPM policies, appoint independent valuers where needed, and ensure methodological consistency.

Investment Monitoring

Monitor sectoral limits, concentration norms, and leverage restrictions. Document all deviations.

Sponsor Commitment

Maintain minimum continuing interest. Reduction below limits affects regulatory standing.

Category I

Venture Capital / SME / Angel

  • Private placement only
  • Min corpus maintenance
  • Quarterly SEBI Reporting
  • Annual Compliance & Financial Audit
  • Sponsor Interest (2.5% or ₹5 Cr)
Category II

Private Equity / Debt Funds

  • No public fund raising
  • No leverage (except operational)
  • Quarterly SEBI Reporting
  • Annual Compliance Test
  • Valuation as per PPM
Category III

Hedge Funds / Long-Short

  • Enhanced PPM Disclosures
  • Strict Leverage Monitoring
  • Frequent Valuation & SEBI Reporting
  • Higher Sponsor Interest (5% or ₹10 Cr)
  • Strong Risk Mgmt Framework

Frequently Asked Questions

FAQs – AIF Compliance, Category-wise Obligations, PPM & Reporting

A. Category-wise AIF Compliance

1. Are compliance requirements different for Category I, II and III AIFs?
Yes. While core governance and reporting obligations apply to all AIFs, Category III AIFs are subject to enhanced risk management, leverage monitoring, and disclosure requirements.
2. What are the key compliance obligations common to all AIF categories?
Common obligations include private placement fund raising, investor KYC, periodic reporting, valuation, audits, disclosure to investors, and regulatory filings.
3. What additional compliance applies specifically to Category I AIFs?
Category I AIFs must comply with sector-specific conditions, especially for venture capital, infrastructure, and social venture investments.
4. Can Category I AIFs receive regulatory incentives?
Yes, certain Category I AIFs may receive regulatory or policy incentives due to their developmental focus, subject to applicable laws.
5. What is the main compliance restriction for Category II AIFs?
Category II AIFs are prohibited from undertaking leverage except for temporary funding requirements for operational needs.
6. Are borrowing and leverage allowed for Category III AIFs?
Yes, Category III AIFs may use leverage, subject to strict disclosure, monitoring, and reporting norms.
7. Is leverage reporting mandatory for Category III AIFs?
Yes, Category III AIFs must continuously monitor and periodically report leverage and exposure details.
8. Do all AIF categories require appointment of a Manager?
Yes, every AIF must have a designated Manager responsible for investment decisions and compliance.
9. Is sponsor continuing interest applicable across all AIF categories?
Yes, but the quantum differs: Category I & II require lower continuing interest compared to Category III.
10. Can an AIF change its category after registration?
Change of category is generally not permitted and would require fresh registration or regulatory approval, depending on circumstances.

B. PPM (Private Placement Memorandum) Compliance

11. What is the regulatory importance of the PPM in an AIF?
The PPM is the primary disclosure document forming the legal and regulatory foundation of the AIF.
12. Is the PPM required to be filed with the regulator?
Yes, the PPM is reviewed during registration and must remain consistent with regulatory disclosures.
13. Can an AIF raise funds without a final PPM?
No. Fund raising cannot commence until the final PPM is issued to investors.
14. What key disclosures must be included in the PPM?
Investment strategy, risk factors, governance framework, fees, conflicts of interest, valuation policy, and investor rights.
15. Are risk disclosures in the PPM mandatory?
Yes. Risks must be clearly and fairly disclosed without understatement.
16. Can the PPM be amended after registration?
Yes, but material changes require investor consent and regulatory intimation or approval.
17. What happens if the PPM and actual operations differ?
Deviation from the PPM may lead to regulatory action, investor disputes, and compliance violations.
18. Is there a separate PPM required for each scheme?
Yes, scheme-specific disclosures must be provided where multiple schemes are launched.
19. Who is responsible for accuracy of PPM disclosures?
The Sponsor and Manager are jointly responsible for the accuracy and completeness of disclosures.
20. Are marketing materials allowed beyond the PPM?
Only factual, non-misleading communication consistent with the PPM is permitted. Public solicitation is prohibited.

C. Fund Raising & Investor Compliance

21. Can an AIF raise funds from the general public?
No. AIFs can raise funds only through private placement.
22. What is the maximum number of investors allowed per scheme?
Each scheme is subject to a prescribed maximum number of investors as per regulations.
23. Is minimum investment requirement mandatory for every investor?
Yes. Minimum investment thresholds apply and must be strictly adhered to.
24. Is investor KYC mandatory before accepting funds?
Yes, KYC and AML checks must be completed before accepting capital commitments.
25. Can AIFs accept foreign investors?
Yes, subject to applicable foreign exchange and regulatory norms.

D. Reporting & Disclosure Obligations

26. What periodic reports must AIFs submit to the regulator?
AIFs must submit quarterly and annual reports covering investments, exposures, and fund activity.
27. Is reporting required even if the fund has not made investments?
Yes, reporting obligations apply regardless of investment activity.
28. Are disclosures to investors mandatory?
Yes, periodic disclosures on portfolio, valuation, and performance must be provided to investors.
29. What valuation frequency applies to AIFs?
Valuation frequency depends on the category and valuation policy disclosed in the PPM.
30. Is independent valuation mandatory?
Independent valuation is required where specified and is considered best practice across categories.

E. Audit & Governance

31. Is annual audit mandatory for AIFs?
Yes, both financial audit and compliance audit are mandatory.
32. Who can conduct the compliance audit of an AIF?
A practising Chartered Accountant, Company Secretary, or Cost Accountant may conduct the audit.
33. Are audit observations required to be reported?
Yes, material observations and corrective actions must be addressed and documented.
34. Is conflict of interest monitoring mandatory?
Yes, conflicts must be identified, managed, and disclosed in line with governance norms.
35. Are related party transactions allowed in AIFs?
They are permitted only if disclosed and managed in accordance with the PPM and regulations.

F. Changes, Events & Regulatory Intimation

36. Does change in Sponsor or Manager require regulatory approval?
Yes, prior approval or immediate intimation is required, depending on the nature of change.
37. Is approval required for change in investment strategy?
Yes, material changes require investor consent and regulatory approval or intimation.
38. What events require immediate regulatory intimation?
Change in control, key personnel, sponsor, manager, or material governance structure.
39. Can an AIF launch multiple schemes under one registration?
Yes, subject to compliance with scheme-level disclosures and reporting.
40. Is scheme tenure extension allowed?
Yes, subject to investor approval and compliance with regulatory norms.

G. Winding-up & Closure

41. What happens when an AIF scheme reaches maturity?
The scheme must be wound up, assets liquidated, and proceeds distributed to investors.
42. Is investor approval required for early winding-up?
Yes, early termination requires investor consent and regulatory reporting.
43. Are final reports required after winding-up?
Yes, final reports must be submitted post-closure.
44. Can an AIF surrender its registration?
Yes, registration may be surrendered subject to settlement of obligations and regulatory approval.

H. Practical Compliance Concerns

45. What are common compliance failures observed in AIFs?
Delayed reporting, PPM deviations, weak disclosures, and governance gaps.
46. Can compliance functions be outsourced?
Yes, but responsibility always remains with the Sponsor and Manager.
47. Are electronic records permitted for AIF compliance?
Yes, provided records are secure, retrievable, and tamper-proof.
48. Is inspection by the regulator possible at any time?
Yes, inspections may be conducted with or without prior notice.
49. What are the consequences of non-compliance by an AIF?
Penalties, restrictions, suspension, or cancellation of registration may be imposed.
50. How can AIFs ensure long-term compliance confidence?
By maintaining strong governance, disciplined reporting, accurate disclosures, and proactive compliance management.
