Understanding Insurance Intermediaries in India

The insurance sector in India is regulated by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI). Insurance intermediaries play a crucial role in connecting insurers with policyholders, and each category requires specific registration and compliance adherence.

Key Regulatory Framework

  • IRDAI (Insurance Brokers) Regulations, 2018
  • IRDAI (Corporate Agents) Regulations, 2015
  • IRDAI (Insurance Repositories) Regulations, 2015
  • Insurance Act, 1938 and amendments

Insurance Broker Registration – Detailed Overview

Broker Categories

Direct Broker (Life)

Specializes in life insurance products and related advisory.

Direct Broker (General)

Focuses on general insurance including health, motor, property, etc.

Composite Broker

Can broker both life and general insurance products.

Reinsurance Broker

Facilitates reinsurance arrangements between insurers and reinsurers.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Entity Type

    Company, LLP, or Partnership Firm registered in India

  • Principal Officer

    Qualified professional with IRDAI-approved certification

  • Net Worth

    Minimum ₹50 lakhs for Direct Broker, ₹75 lakhs for Composite Broker

  • Professional Indemnity

    Mandatory PI insurance coverage as per IRDAI norms

  • Infrastructure

    Office space, IT systems, and operational capabilities

Frequently Asked Questions

Common queries about insurance intermediary registration and compliance

What is the difference between an Insurance Broker and a Corporate Agent?
An Insurance Broker acts as an independent intermediary representing the client's interests and can work with multiple insurers across categories. A Corporate Agent represents specific insurance companies (maximum 3 per category) and acts on behalf of the insurer. Brokers have higher capital requirements and broader operational flexibility, while Corporate Agents have simpler entry requirements but limited insurer partnerships.
How long does it take to get IRDAI registration as an Insurance Broker?
The typical timeline is 8-12 weeks from application submission, assuming all documentation is complete and accurate. This includes entity setup (2-3 weeks), application preparation (1 week), IRDAI review (4-8 weeks), and final approval (1-2 weeks). Timelines may vary based on the complexity of your application and IRDAI's workload.
What are the capital requirements for different insurance intermediary categories?
For Insurance Brokers: Direct Broker (Life/General) requires ₹50 lakhs net worth, Composite Broker requires ₹75 lakhs, and Reinsurance Broker requires ₹2 crores. For Corporate Agents: Security deposit of ₹2 lakhs for single category (Life/General/Health) and ₹4 lakhs for Composite Corporate Agent. Additionally, Professional Indemnity Insurance is mandatory for brokers.
Can an individual become an Insurance Broker or Corporate Agent?
No, IRDAI regulations require Insurance Brokers and Corporate Agents to be registered as companies, LLPs, or partnership firms. Individuals cannot directly obtain these licenses. However, individuals can become licensed insurance agents (individual agents) by obtaining certification and working under an insurance company.
What certifications are required for the Principal Officer?
The Principal Officer must pass the IRDAI-approved examination conducted by the Insurance Institute of India (III) or other designated bodies. For brokers, the IC-38 (General Insurance) and IC-33 (Life Insurance) certifications are typically required. For Corporate Agents, similar certifications based on the category (Life/General/Health) are mandatory. The certification must be valid and renewed periodically.
Can a Corporate Agent work with multiple insurance companies?
Yes, but with limitations. A Corporate Agent can tie up with a maximum of three insurance companies in each category (Life, General, Health). For example, a Composite Corporate Agent can work with up to 3 life insurers, 3 general insurers, and 3 health insurers simultaneously. This is different from Insurance Brokers who can work with unlimited insurers.
What is an Insurance Repository and who needs it?
An Insurance Repository is a digital platform that stores insurance policies in electronic (dematerialized) form, similar to how Demat accounts work for securities. Policyholders can hold all their insurance policies in a single electronic account. Setting up an Insurance Repository requires IRDAI registration and is typically undertaken by technology companies or financial institutions looking to provide digital insurance infrastructure services.
What are the ongoing compliance requirements after registration?
Key ongoing requirements include: (1) Annual license renewal with IRDAI, (2) Maintenance of minimum net worth/capital adequacy, (3) Quarterly and annual regulatory filings, (4) Professional Indemnity Insurance renewal (for brokers), (5) Continuing education and certification for principal officers and staff, (6) Maintenance of proper books of accounts and client records, (7) Compliance with IRDAI's code of conduct, and (8) Timely payment of regulatory fees.
Can foreign entities become Insurance Brokers or Corporate Agents in India?
Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) is permitted in insurance intermediaries subject to IRDAI and FEMA regulations. Currently, 100% FDI is allowed in insurance intermediaries under the automatic route. However, the entity must be incorporated in India and comply with all IRDAI registration requirements, including having a Principal Officer who is a resident Indian with the required certifications.
What happens if I operate as an insurance intermediary without IRDAI registration?
Operating as an insurance intermediary without proper IRDAI registration is illegal and can result in severe penalties including: (1) Monetary fines up to ₹25 lakhs, (2) Imprisonment up to 3 years, (3) Permanent ban from the insurance industry, (4) Legal action by IRDAI, and (5) Liability for any client losses. IRDAI actively monitors and penalizes unregistered entities, especially those operating digitally or through social media.
Do I need separate registrations for Life and General insurance?
It depends on the category you choose. For Insurance Brokers, you can opt for Direct Broker (Life only), Direct Broker (General only), or Composite Broker (both Life and General). For Corporate Agents, you can choose Life, General, Health, or Composite categories. If you want to operate in multiple categories, a Composite license is more efficient than obtaining separate registrations, though it has higher capital requirements.
How does Professional Indemnity Insurance work for Insurance Brokers?
Professional Indemnity (PI) Insurance is mandatory for Insurance Brokers to protect against claims arising from professional negligence or errors. The minimum PI coverage required is based on the broker's annual gross revenue: ₹50 lakhs for revenue up to ₹10 crores, ₹1 crore for revenue between ₹10-50 crores, and higher amounts for larger operations. The PI policy must be maintained continuously and renewed before expiry.
