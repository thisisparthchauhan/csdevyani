What Is an Alternative Investment Fund (AIF)?

An Alternative Investment Fund is a privately pooled investment vehicle that collects funds from eligible investors and invests according to a defined investment policy for the benefit of its investors.

Regulated separately from mutual funds, AIFs are designed for sophisticated investors and enable customised investment strategies.

Typical Use Cases

  • Private Equity & Venture Capital
  • Start-up & Growth Funding
  • Real Estate & Infrastructure
  • Distressed Assets
  • Hedge Funds & Derivatives
  • Special Situations

Categories of AIFs

SEBI classifies AIFs into three distinct categories based on strategy and regulatory intent.

I

Category I AIF

Invests in socially or economically desirable sectors. Often enjoys incentives.

  • • Venture Capital Funds
  • • SME Funds
  • • Social Venture Funds
  • • Infrastructure Funds
  • • Angel Funds
II

Category II AIF

Funds that do not undertake leverage except for day-to-day operations. Most widely used.

  • • Private Equity Funds
  • • Debt Funds
  • • Fund of Funds
  • • Real Estate Funds
III

Category III AIF

Funds employing complex trading strategies, including leverage.

  • • Hedge Funds
  • • Long-Short Funds
  • • Arbitrage Funds
  • • Complex Derivative Strategies

Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers to common queries on AIF Registration.

What is the minimum corpus for an AIF?
The minimum corpus for each scheme of an AIF is ₹20 Crore (₹10 Crore for Angel Funds).
Can an AIF accept funds from foreign investors?
Yes, AIFs can accept funds from foreign investors, subject to FEMA guidelines and RBI reporting.
How long does AIF registration take?
The process typically takes 3-6 months, depending on the clarity of the application, PPM quality, and response time to SEBI queries.
Is a physical office mandatory?
While SEBI regulations focus on infrastructure, a registered office in India is required for the entity (Trust/LLP/Company).
What is the role of a Sponsor?
The Sponsor sets up the AIF and is required to maintain a continuing interest (skin-in-the-game) in the fund to align interests with investors.
Can AIFs raise funds from the public?
No, AIFs can only raise funds through private placement to sophisticated investors. Public solicitation is strictly prohibited.
