What Is an Alternative Investment Fund (AIF)?

An Alternative Investment Fund is a privately pooled investment vehicle that collects funds from eligible investors and invests according to a defined investment policy for the benefit of its investors.

Regulated separately from mutual funds, AIFs are designed for sophisticated investors and enable customised investment strategies.

Typical Use Cases

  • Private Equity & Venture Capital
  • Start-up & Growth Funding
  • Real Estate & Infrastructure
  • Distressed Assets
  • Hedge Funds & Derivatives
  • Special Situations

Categories of AIFs

SEBI classifies AIFs into three distinct categories based on strategy and regulatory intent.

I

Category I AIF

Invests in socially or economically desirable sectors. Often enjoys incentives.

  • • Venture Capital Funds
  • • SME Funds
  • • Social Venture Funds
  • • Infrastructure Funds
  • • Angel Funds
II

Category II AIF

Funds that do not undertake leverage except for day-to-day operations. Most widely used.

  • • Private Equity Funds
  • • Debt Funds
  • • Fund of Funds
  • • Real Estate Funds
III

Category III AIF

Funds employing complex trading strategies, including leverage.

  • • Hedge Funds
  • • Long-Short Funds
  • • Arbitrage Funds
  • • Complex Derivative Strategies

Advanced FAQs – Alternative Investment Fund (AIF) Registration in India

Comprehensive answers to critical regulatory, structuring, and operational queries.

A. Foundational & Structuring Questions

1. Can an AIF be registered before identifying investors?
Yes. AIF registration is obtained before fund raising. Investors are onboarded only after registration and issuance of the final PPM.
2. Is it mandatory to raise the full corpus immediately after registration?
No. The minimum corpus requirement applies to the fund overall, not to immediate fund raising.
3. Can an AIF be registered with a small initial team?
Yes, provided the Manager demonstrates adequate experience, infrastructure, and operational capability.
4. Is a trust structure compulsory for AIF registration?
No. AIFs may be structured as a trust, LLP, or company, though trusts are most commonly preferred.
5. Can the same entity act as Sponsor and Manager?
Yes, subject to proper disclosure and compliance with continuing interest requirements.
6. Is prior fund management experience mandatory for AIF registration?
Direct experience is not mandatory, but SEBI evaluates collective experience, governance strength, and professional capability.
7. Can first-time fund managers obtain AIF registration?
Yes, if supported by a robust governance framework, experienced professionals, and clear investment strategy.
8. Can an NBFC sponsor or manage an AIF?
Yes, subject to compliance with applicable RBI and SEBI norms and proper segregation of activities.
9. Can a family office register an AIF?
Yes, many family offices use the AIF structure for pooled investment, subject to eligibility and compliance.
10. Is it possible to register multiple AIFs under the same Sponsor?
Yes, provided each AIF meets independent eligibility, governance, and compliance requirements.

B. Category-Specific & Strategy Questions

11. Can the same investment strategy fit into more than one AIF category?
Some strategies may overlap, but the chosen category must align clearly with regulatory definitions.
12. Is leverage completely prohibited for Category II AIFs?
Leverage is prohibited except for temporary funding for operational requirements, subject to limits.
13. Why does Category III AIF face higher regulatory scrutiny?
Because it permits leverage and complex strategies, increasing systemic and investor risk.
14. Can a debt fund be registered under Category I?
No. Debt strategies typically fall under Category II.
15. Can a venture capital strategy be registered as Category II?
No. Venture capital strategies fall under Category I by definition.
16. Can an AIF change its category after registration?
Generally no. A category change may require fresh registration.
17. Can one AIF launch schemes with different strategies?
Yes, subject to scheme-level disclosures and compliance with the approved category.
18. Is it permissible to invest outside India through an AIF?
Yes, subject to foreign exchange laws and disclosures in the PPM.
19. Can an AIF invest in unlisted companies only?
No. Investment scope depends on the strategy disclosed in the PPM.
20. Can a Category III AIF invest in listed securities actively?
Yes, subject to strategy, disclosures, and risk management framework.

C. PPM (Private Placement Memorandum) – Complex Issues

21. Is the PPM legally binding on the AIF?
Yes. The AIF must operate strictly in accordance with the disclosures made in the PPM.
22. Can generic or template PPMs be used for AIF registration?
No. Generic PPMs often lead to regulatory queries and rejection.
23. Is investor consent required for every PPM amendment?
Only for material changes affecting investment strategy, fees, or rights.
24. Can marketing presentations override the PPM?
No. Any communication inconsistent with the PPM is a compliance violation.
25. Is a separate PPM required for each scheme?
Yes, scheme-specific disclosures are mandatory.
26. Who bears liability for incorrect disclosures in the PPM?
The Sponsor and Manager are jointly responsible.
27. Can fees be changed after registration?
Only with proper disclosure, investor consent, and regulatory compliance.
28. Can risk disclosures be brief if investors are sophisticated?
No. Full and fair disclosure is mandatory regardless of investor sophistication.
29. Is SEBI approval required for every PPM update?
Material changes require intimation or approval, depending on impact.
30. Can PPMs be shared publicly?
No. PPMs are private placement documents and cannot be publicly circulated.

D. Investor & Fund Raising Questions

31. Can an AIF raise funds before completing KYC?
No. Investor onboarding must be completed before accepting commitments.
32. Can an investor invest less than ₹1 crore through structuring?
No. Minimum investment norms cannot be circumvented.
33. Can employees of the AIF invest at a lower threshold?
Yes, subject to prescribed limits and disclosures.
34. Is there a cap on the number of investors in an AIF scheme?
Yes. Each scheme is subject to a maximum investor limit.
35. Can foreign investors participate in an AIF?
Yes, subject to foreign exchange and regulatory compliance.
36. Can AIF units be transferred between investors?
Yes, subject to PPM terms and regulatory conditions.
37. Can AIFs accept capital in tranches?
Yes, capital commitments may be drawn down as per fund terms.
38. Is escrow mandatory for fund collection?
Operational safeguards are expected, and escrow mechanisms are commonly used.
39. Can an AIF advertise to attract investors?
No. Public solicitation and advertising are prohibited.
40. Can placement agents be appointed for fund raising?
Yes, subject to disclosure and regulatory compliance.

E. Registration Process & Regulatory Scrutiny

41. Can SEBI ask for changes in fund strategy during registration?
Yes. SEBI may seek clarification or alignment with regulatory intent.
42. Is registration guaranteed if eligibility criteria are met?
No. SEBI retains discretionary powers to assess overall suitability.
43. Can SEBI reject an AIF application without detailed reasons?
SEBI generally provides reasons or seeks clarification before rejection.
44. Can an applicant withdraw an AIF application?
Yes, subject to procedural formalities. Fees are non-refundable.
45. How long does AIF registration typically take?
Timelines vary based on documentation quality and regulatory queries.
46. Can multiple applications be filed simultaneously?
Yes, but each application is assessed independently.
47. Is in-person interaction with SEBI mandatory?
Not always, but SEBI may call for meetings or clarifications.
48. Can professional advisors represent the applicant before SEBI?
Yes, authorised professionals may represent the applicant.
49. Can an AIF start operations immediately after registration?
Yes, subject to completion of operational and compliance readiness.
50. Is registration transferable to another entity?
No. AIF registration is entity-specific and non-transferable.

F. Practical & Risk-Oriented Questions

51. What is the biggest reason for AIF application delays?
Weak PPM drafting and inconsistent disclosures.
52. What common mistake first-time fund managers make?
Underestimating governance and compliance expectations.
53. Can poor compliance post-registration impact fund raising?
Yes. Investor confidence and regulatory standing are directly affected.
54. Can an AIF be penalised for actions of the Manager?
Yes. The AIF is responsible for actions taken on its behalf.
55. Can an AIF operate without raising funds for a long period?
Yes, but reporting and compliance obligations continue.
56. Is regulatory inspection common for AIFs?
Inspections may be conducted based on risk, complaints, or routine supervision.
57. Can an AIF surrender registration voluntarily?
Yes, subject to settlement of obligations and regulatory approval.
58. Can AIF registration be cancelled by the regulator?
Yes, in cases of serious or repeated non-compliance.
59. Does AIF registration enhance credibility with institutional investors?
Yes. Registration provides regulatory credibility and governance assurance.
60. Is professional advisory essential for AIF registration?
While not mandatory, professional structuring significantly improves approval certainty and long-term compliance.
61. Is AIF registration suitable for small ticket funds?
AIFs are designed for sophisticated investors and may not suit small retail-focused models.
62. Can an AIF later convert into a mutual fund?
No. Mutual funds require separate regulatory approval and structure.
63. Is AIF registration suitable for fintech investment platforms?
Yes, if the platform operates within the private placement and advisory framework.
64. Can an AIF co-invest with other funds?
Yes, subject to disclosure and conflict management norms.
65. What is the most critical success factor for AIF registration?
Clear strategy, strong governance, accurate disclosures, and disciplined compliance.
