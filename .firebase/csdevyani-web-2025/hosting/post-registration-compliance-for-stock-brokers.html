Ongoing Compliance

Post-Registration Compliance
for Stock Brokers (SEBI)

Once Stock Broker Registration with SEBI is granted, the broker becomes a continuously regulated intermediary. Compliance is not event-based; it is ongoing, auditable, and enforceable.

Get Compliance Support

Continuous Adherence

All obligations arise from SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations, 1992, SEBI Act, 1992, Stock Exchange bye-laws, and Clearing Corporation requirements. Failure at any stage can lead to inspection findings, penalties, suspension, or cancellation by SEBI.

  • Maintaining valid exchange membership at all times
  • Obtaining prior SEBI approval for any change in control
  • Continuous maintenance of prescribed net worth
  • Adherence to SEBI Code of Conduct
  • Timely redressal of investor grievances

Maintenance of Books, Records & Documents

Statutory Records

  • Client master records and KYC documents
  • Client agreements and risk disclosures
  • Order books, trade books, and sauda books
  • Client ledger and margin records
  • Bank account and demat reconciliations
  • Contract notes and trade confirmations
  • Proprietary trading records

Record Retention

All records must be preserved for a **minimum period of five years**, either in physical or electronic form, and must be readily producible during inspections.

Electronic records must be secure, retrievable, and tamper-proof.

Client Onboarding & KYC Compliance

Before executing any trade via a client, the broker must ensure:

Completion of KYC as per prescribed norms

Execution of client–broker agreement

Acknowledgement of risk disclosure documents

Allotment of Unique Client Code (UCC)

Verification of client bank and demat accounts

Trading without proper KYC is a serious regulatory violation.

Segregation of Client Funds & Securities

Strict Segregation

  • Maintain separate bank accounts for client funds and proprietary funds
  • Ensure client securities are segregated from proprietary holdings
  • Comply with daily and periodic client fund reporting

Misuse or diversion of client funds attracts severe enforcement action.

Margin Collection & Risk Management

Collect Margins

Collect margins as prescribed by exchanges

Risk Monitoring

Implement real-time risk monitoring systems

Square Off

Square off positions in case of margin shortfall

Exposure Limits

Maintain exposure limits for clients and prop trades

Failure in margin collection is treated as a systemic risk issue.

Appointment of Compliance Officer

Every stock broker must appoint a Compliance Officer who is functionally independent.

  • Monitors adherence to SEBI and exchange regulations
  • Reports non-compliance directly to the Board
  • Oversees investor grievance redressal
  • Acts as nodal point during inspections

Investor Grievance Redressal

Failure to resolve grievances attracts penalties and inspection observations.

  • Maintain a formal grievance redressal mechanism
  • Resolve investor complaints within prescribed timelines
  • Upload complaints and resolutions on exchange platforms
  • Escalate unresolved grievances appropriately

Reporting, Audits & Tech Controls

Periodic Reporting

  • • Net worth certificates
  • • Client fund and securities statements
  • • Margin and exposure reports
  • • Financial statements and audit reports
  • • Compliance and risk management reports
  • • Surveillance reports

Annual & Periodic Audits

  • • Internal audit
  • • System audit (including cyber security)
  • • Compliance audit
  • • Statutory financial audit

Audit observations must be documented with corrective action reports.

Cyber Security

  • • Approved trading and risk systems
  • • Cyber security framework
  • • Access controls and data protection
  • • Business continuity and DR plans

Technology failures are treated as operational risk events.

Inspection & Enforcement

SEBI and stock exchanges have statutory powers to conduct inspections with or without notice.

  • • Examine books, systems, and records
  • • Call for explanations from directors/staff
  • • Non-cooperation is a violation

Penalties

SEBI may impose monetary penalties, suspension, or cancellation of registration.

Enforcement action is based on objective compliance failures, not intent.

Change Management & Regulatory Intimations

Prior approval or immediate intimation is required for changes in shareholding/control, directors/key management, compliance officer, or business model.

Registration is only the starting point. Compliance determines survival.

Stock Broker Compliance (SEBI) – FAQs

Comprehensive answers to common compliance queries.

A. General Compliance Obligations

What are the core compliance obligations of a stock broker after registration?
A stock broker must continuously comply with SEBI regulations, exchange bye-laws, circulars, conditions of registration, investor protection norms, and risk management requirements throughout its operations.
Is compliance a one-time requirement after registration?
No. Compliance is ongoing and continuous. Registration only marks the beginning of regulatory supervision.
What is the Code of Conduct applicable to stock brokers?
Stock brokers must act with integrity, fairness, professionalism, due skill, and care, and must not indulge in fraudulent, manipulative, or misleading practices.
Are SEBI circulars mandatory for stock brokers?
Yes. SEBI circulars and exchange circulars issued pursuant to SEBI directions are binding and must be complied with until withdrawn.
Can a broker ignore exchange circulars if not issued directly by SEBI?
No. Exchange circulars issued under SEBI’s regulatory framework are enforceable and mandatory.

B. Books, Records & Documentation

What books and records must a stock broker maintain?
Stock brokers must maintain client master data, KYC documents, trade records, order logs, contract notes, client ledgers, margin records, bank and demat reconciliations, and complaint records.
For how long must records be preserved?
Records must generally be preserved for a minimum of five years or such longer period as may be specified.
Can brokers maintain records in electronic form?
Yes, provided records are secure, retrievable, tamper-proof, and available for inspection.
Is segregation of client and proprietary records mandatory?
Yes. Client funds and securities must be segregated from proprietary funds and securities at all times.
Is maintenance of an investor complaint register compulsory?
Yes. All complaints, resolutions, and escalation details must be recorded and tracked.

C. Compliance Officer & Internal Controls

Is appointment of a Compliance Officer mandatory?
Yes. Every stock broker must appoint a Compliance Officer responsible for regulatory compliance.
What are the responsibilities of a Compliance Officer?
Monitoring compliance, reporting violations, overseeing grievance redressal, coordinating audits, and acting as liaison with regulators and exchanges.
Can the Compliance Officer perform operational roles?
The Compliance Officer should be functionally independent; excessive operational involvement is discouraged.
Is a whistle-blower mechanism required?
Yes. Brokers must have a mechanism for confidential reporting of unethical or non-compliant conduct.
Are internal surveillance systems mandatory?
Yes. Brokers must monitor trading activity to detect fraud, manipulation, and abnormal patterns.

D. Client-Related Compliance

What compliance is required before onboarding a client?
Completion of KYC, execution of client agreement, risk disclosure acknowledgment, and verification of bank and demat accounts.
Are brokers required to issue contract notes?
Yes. Contract notes must be issued within prescribed timelines for every trade executed.
Is settlement compliance mandatory?
Yes. Timely settlement of securities and funds as per exchange clearing and settlement cycles is mandatory.
Can a broker retain client funds beyond prescribed timelines?
No. Retention of client funds without regulatory basis is a serious violation.
Are periodic client statements required?
Yes. Clients must receive periodic statements of funds and securities balances.

E. Risk Management & Financial Compliance

Are risk management systems mandatory for stock brokers?
Yes. Brokers must have systems to monitor exposure, margins, and market risks.
Is margin collection a compliance requirement?
Yes. Prescribed margins must be collected, monitored, and enforced.
Can brokers use client funds for proprietary obligations?
No. Client funds cannot be used for proprietary trading or obligations.
Is net worth reporting part of compliance?
Yes. Brokers must periodically certify and report net worth as required.
Are cyber security norms applicable to stock brokers?
Yes. Brokers must implement cyber security and cyber resilience frameworks.

F. Reporting Obligations

What periodic reports must stock brokers submit?
Compliance reports, financial statements, net worth certificates, client fund reports, audit reports, and surveillance reports.
Is consolidated reporting permitted for brokers operating on multiple exchanges?
Yes, subject to prescribed mechanisms and regulatory approval.
Must suspicious transactions be reported?
Yes. Any suspicious or potentially manipulative activity must be reported promptly.
Are system failures required to be reported?
Yes. Major technology failures or disruptions must be reported to exchanges and regulators.
Are inspection observations binding?
Yes. Brokers must address inspection findings within stipulated timelines.

G. Surveillance & Market Abuse Prevention

Are brokers required to monitor unusual trading activity?
Yes. Ongoing monitoring of trading patterns is mandatory.
Is escalation of irregular activity required?
Yes. Suspicious activity must be escalated internally and reported externally where required.
Are brokers responsible for monitoring client behaviour?
Yes. Brokers must monitor for abnormal or potentially abusive client behaviour.
Is a formal market abuse prevention framework required?
Yes. Policies and procedures must exist to prevent fraud and market abuse.

H. Investor Protection & Conduct

Are brokers required to protect investor interests?
Yes. Investor protection is a core regulatory objective.
Are timelines prescribed for grievance resolution?
Yes. Complaints must be resolved within prescribed timelines.
Are advertising and marketing regulated for brokers?
Yes. Brokers must not issue misleading or unauthorised advertisements.
Can brokers offer inducements to attract clients?
No. Unfair inducements and misrepresentations are prohibited.

I. Inspections & Enforcement

Can regulators inspect a broker without notice?
Yes. Inspections may be conducted with or without prior notice.
What happens if inspection observations are ignored?
Non-compliance may lead to penalties, restrictions, or suspension.
Can SEBI impose penalties for compliance failures?
Yes. Monetary penalties, operational restrictions, or registration action may be imposed.
Is misuse of client funds a serious violation?
Yes. It is among the gravest compliance breaches.

J. Corporate & Governance Compliance

Does the Board have responsibility for compliance?
Yes. The Board must oversee compliance and risk management.
Are audit committees required for large brokers?
Yes. Larger brokers are expected to have structured governance committees.
What is a Qualified Stock Broker (QSB)?
A broker designated for enhanced regulatory oversight due to scale and systemic importance.

K. Penalties & Corrective Measures

Can SEBI restrict business activities for non-compliance?
Yes. Restrictions may include client onboarding bans or trading limits.
What triggers suspension of broker registration?
Persistent non-compliance, serious violations, or failure to rectify deficiencies.
Are corrective action plans mandatory after inspections?
Yes. Brokers must implement and report corrective measures.
Can compliance failures impact exchange membership?
Yes. Exchanges may impose restrictions or recommend regulatory action.
Is compliance training of staff mandatory?
Yes. Staff must be trained on regulatory obligations and compliance standards.
Are internal audits part of compliance?
Yes. Internal and system audits are mandatory.
Is segregation of client funds reviewed during audits?
Yes. This is a key audit and inspection focus area.
Are compliance reports subject to verification?
Yes. Reports may be verified during inspections and audits.
Can compliance obligations differ by broker category?
Yes. Obligations may vary based on broker type and scale.
Does non-compliance affect reputation and credibility?
Yes. Regulatory actions are public and impact market standing.
Is compliance outsourcing permitted?
Certain functions may be outsourced, but responsibility remains with the broker.
Must brokers document compliance processes?
Yes. Written policies and procedures are mandatory.
Are brokers required to update policies regularly?
Yes. Policies must reflect regulatory changes.
Is compliance review a continuous process?
Yes. Compliance is dynamic and ongoing.
Why is strong compliance critical for stock brokers?
Because regulatory trust, operational continuity, and investor confidence depend on it.
