Post-Registration Compliance

Depository Participant
Compliance & Reporting

Once registered, a DP becomes a systemically important intermediary. Compliance is continuous, auditable, and enforceable by SEBI and depositories (NSDL/CDSL).

Get Compliance Support

Most regulatory actions against DPs arise due to post-registration lapses—especially in asset segregation, KYC norms, grievance handling, and technology controls.

Core Regulatory Obligations

Net Worth Discipline

Maintain prescribed net worth continuously. Restore shortfall immediately.

Client Onboarding & KYC

Complete KYC, execute agreements, and verify bank details before account opening.

Asset Segregation

Strict separation of client securities and DP's own assets. No unauthorized pledges.

Operations & Records

Maintain accurate transaction logs, master data, and audit trails.

Grievance Redressal

Time-bound resolution of complaints with proper documentation and reporting.

Fees Transparency

Disclose all charges. No hidden or unauthorized fees.

Tech & Cyber Security

Secure IT systems, data encryption, backups, and BCP/DR readiness.

Regulatory Reporting

Timely submission of compliance confirmations, net worth, and event based reports.

Audits & Inspections

Internal/Concurrent audits and cooperation during SEBI/Depository inspections.

Change Management

Prior approval/intimation for changes in control, directors, or operations.

Compliance Officer

Dedicated officer ensuring functional independence and Board reporting.

Enforcement Awareness

Preventive compliance to avoid penalties, restrictions, or suspension.

DP Compliance – FAQs

Expert answers to common regulatory questions for Depository Participants.

Is DP compliance mandatory after registration?
Yes. DP registration carries continuous compliance obligations.
Who is responsible for DP compliance internally?
The Compliance Officer, with oversight from senior management and the Board.
Can DP compliance be handled casually?
No. SEBI expects documented and auditable compliance systems.
Is minimum net worth required after DP registration?
Yes. Prescribed net worth must be maintained at all times.
What happens if net worth falls below the limit?
The shortfall must be restored immediately to avoid regulatory action.
Is net worth checked during inspections?
Yes. Net worth is a key inspection parameter.
Is KYC mandatory before opening a demat account?
Yes. No account can be opened without complete KYC.
Can DP open accounts with partial documentation?
No. Incomplete onboarding is a violation.
Is verification of bank details mandatory?
Yes. Bank verification is essential for investor protection.
Must client securities be segregated from DP assets?
Yes. Segregation is a fundamental compliance requirement.
Can DP use client securities for its own purposes?
No. This is strictly prohibited.
Is misuse of securities a serious offence?
Yes. It attracts severe regulatory action.
What records must a DP maintain?
Client data, transaction logs, demat/remat records, pledges, and audit trails.
For how long must records be preserved?
As per prescribed retention timelines; records must be retrievable.
Are electronic records allowed?
Yes, provided they are secure and auditable.
Is grievance redressal mandatory for DPs?
Yes. DPs must have a formal grievance mechanism.
Are grievance timelines monitored by regulators?
Yes. Delays attract scrutiny.
Must all complaints be documented?
Yes, regardless of mode of receipt.
Must DP disclose all charges to clients?
Yes. Fee transparency is mandatory.
Can DP charge fees not approved by depository?
No. Only approved charges may be levied.
Are hidden charges permitted?
No. Hidden fees are prohibited.
Are IT systems critical for DP compliance?
Yes. Secure systems are essential.
Is data backup mandatory?
Yes. Data integrity and recovery are crucial.
Is disaster recovery planning required?
Yes. BCP and DR arrangements are expected.
Are periodic reports mandatory?
Yes. DPs must submit prescribed reports within timelines.
Is delay in reporting treated seriously?
Yes. Timely reporting is essential.
Must errors in submissions be corrected?
Yes. Immediate correction and disclosure are required.
Are DPs subject to inspections?
Yes. Depositories and SEBI may conduct inspections.
Can inspections be unannounced?
Yes. Surprise inspections are permitted.
Are inspection observations binding?
Yes. Corrective action is mandatory.
Is appointment of a Compliance Officer mandatory?
Yes. A designated Compliance Officer is compulsory.
Can compliance responsibility be outsourced?
Certain tasks may be outsourced, but accountability remains with DP.
Is Board oversight required?
Yes. Compliance is a governance responsibility.
Is prior approval required for change in control?
Yes. Prior regulatory approval is mandatory.
Are changes in directors reportable?
Yes. Immediate disclosure is required.
Can DP change business model freely?
No. Material changes require regulatory review.
Can DP operations be suspended?
Yes. Serious non-compliance can lead to suspension.
Can registration be cancelled?
Yes, in cases of persistent or grave violations.
Are penalties imposed for lapses?
Yes. Monetary and operational penalties may apply.
What is the most common DP compliance failure?
Weak grievance handling and poor record management.
Does strong compliance improve investor confidence?
Yes. Compliance builds trust.
Is compliance costly for DPs?
Compliance is an investment in stability.
Can small DPs ignore compliance rigor?
No. Regulations apply uniformly.
Are compliance failures publicly visible?
Yes. Regulatory actions affect reputation.
Can good compliance reduce inspection risk?
Yes. Structured compliance lowers regulatory exposure.
Is DP compliance a one-time exercise?
No. It is continuous.
Are internal SOPs expected?
Yes. SOPs demonstrate control maturity.
Is staff training important for DP compliance?
Yes. Awareness reduces operational errors.
Can DP expand services after registration?
Yes, subject to regulatory permissions.
What is the safest compliance approach for DPs?
Clear processes, regular reviews, and professional oversight.
Why is DP compliance treated seriously by regulators?
Because DPs safeguard investor securities and trust.
